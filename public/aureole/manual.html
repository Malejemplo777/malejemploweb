Quick-start guide
AUREOLE.650

Real film halation for DaVinci Resolve — a soft contour bleed on marked edges, and a diffuse glow around bright sources, in one node.

by Malejemplo

01

Put it in the right space

AUREOLE does its math on scene-referred light, in DaVinci Wide Gamut Intermediate — never on Rec.709 or any already-tonemapped image. One node, one fixed spot in the chain.

Never after the output conversion. Once the image is tonemapped to Rec.709, the real brightness AUREOLE needs is already gone.
02

Applying it

1
Color page Open the clip, already converted to DWGI.
2
OpenFX → Malejemplo Find AUREOLE.650 in the effects list.
3
Drop it right after DWGI Before your output conversion, see 01.
03

The panel

Four groups, top to bottom in the order you actually use them. Preview first — calibrate without guessing.

Preview Halation Mask, Glow Mask and Affected Zone — see what counts as source before you trust the result.
General Glow A wide, diffuse halo around extended bright areas — its own threshold, intensity and size.
Edge Halation A contour ring along marked edges — its own threshold, strength, size and detail.
Advanced Hue and Saturation of the Edge Halation color — closed by default, only for when Standard doesn't fit.
04

Remember

General Glow and Edge Halation never share a threshold. Calibrate each in its own Preview mode — fixing one never breaks the other.
Go easy on Glow Intensity over dense water glitter. AUREOLE protects near-white highlights, but very busy sparkle can still pick up a touch of warmth. Check the result at full size.
Keep one video track active while you work. Resolve still evaluates stacked tracks below even when hidden — turn off what you're not looking at.
